## The Specs: What We Are Dealing With

You cannot judge a frame without checking the numbers. I weighed my freshly strung retail racket to give you accurate data. 

The strung weight comes in at 318 grams. It features a 68 RA stiffness rating. This puts it firmly in the "stiff" category, right alongside the Babolat Pure Drive. The defining feature is the 100-square-inch Isometric head shape. Yonex slightly squares off the top of the hoop. This unique geometry physically expands the sweet spot outward toward the frame edges. 

You can review all the technical specifications directly on the [Yonex official website](https://www.yonex.com). 

## Groundstrokes: The Rocket Launcher

When you swing the EZONE 100 from the baseline, you instantly feel the free power. 

The sweet spot feels absolutely massive. I frequently hit forehands late during defensive sprints. Even when the ball caught the upper edge of the strings, the frame launched it deep into the opponent's court. The 2G-Namd Flex Force graphite snaps back violently. This trampoline effect pushes your opponent backward immediately.

However, this raw power comes with a strict warning. If you have a very flat swing, this racket will punish you. When I forgot to brush up on the ball, my flat drives sailed three feet past the baseline. You must use topspin to control the trajectory. If you struggle to generate spin, check our guide on the [best beginner tennis rackets](/posts/best-beginner-tennis-racket) for more forgiving options. 

## Serving: Free Points

This is my favorite aspect of this Yonex Ezone 100 review. Serving with this racket feels entirely unfair to the returner. 

When you hit a flat first serve, the stiff frame transfers energy perfectly into the ball. The pace is overwhelming. I found myself hitting clean aces down the T with minimal physical effort. 

The 16x19 string pattern also grips the ball beautifully for kick serves. My second serves bounced aggressively high above the returner's shoulder. If you need a weapon to hold serve more consistently, this frame delivers immediately. 

## Volleys: Fast but Unstable

At the net, the EZONE 100 shines in fast exchanges. The aerodynamic beam cuts through the air quickly. I easily snapped my wrist to execute sharp reaction volleys during doubles play. 

The major drawback emerges when you face heavy passing shots. The frame lacks the plow-through weight of a heavier control racket. When my opponent blasted a 90 mph passing shot directly at me, the EZONE twisted noticeably in my hand. You have to grip the handle tightly to prevent torsional instability on off-center blocks. 

If you prefer a heavier, more stable frame at the net, read our [Yonex Ezone 98 review](/posts/yonex-ezone-98-review) to compare. 

## Comfort and Feel

Stiff power frames usually cause tennis elbow. Yonex combats this by placing Vibration Dampening Mesh inside the handle. 

Does it work? Yes. I compared the EZONE 100 directly against a Babolat Pure Drive. The Pure Drive feels incredibly harsh on mishits. The EZONE feels remarkably plush by comparison. The dampening mesh filters out the high-frequency vibrations before they reach your wrist. It is undoubtedly the most comfortable power frame currently available.

## FAQs

### Is the Yonex EZONE 100 good for tennis elbow?

Yes. While it is technically a stiff power frame (68 RA), the internal dampening technology makes it much safer than its direct competitors. However, always use soft strings if you have existing pain.

### What is the difference between the EZONE 98 and EZONE 100?

The EZONE 100 provides more power, a larger sweet spot, and easier playability for intermediates. The EZONE 98 offers a smaller head, a thinner beam, and far more control for advanced players who supply their own pace.

### What strings work best in the EZONE 100?

To tame the massive power, I recommend stringing a polyester like Yonex Poly Tour Pro at 52 pounds. If you prioritize comfort over spin, use a high-quality multifilament at 55 pounds.
