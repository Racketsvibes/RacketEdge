nnnnnnnnnnnnnnnnnn## **Buying Guide**

nnnn### **How to Choose an Arm-Friendly Racket**

nnnn*The wrong specs will hurt you. Here is what to look for.*

nnnn### **1. Stiffness Rating (RA)**

nnnnThis is the most important number.

nnnn
n- **High Stiffness (69+ RA):** Like a brick wall. Great for power, bad for elbows.nnnn- **Low Stiffness (<65 RA):** Like a trampoline. It bends to absorb shock.nnnn- *Recommendation:* Aim for an RA of **65 or lower**. (The Wilson Clash is ~57).n
nnnn### **2. Weight: Heavy is Good?**

nnnnYes. A heavier racket is more stable. If you use a featherweight racket (under 285g), the ball will push the racket around, twisting your wrist and elbow. A heavier racket (300g+) "plows through" the ball so your arm doesn't take the hit.

nnnn### **3. String Choice Matters MORE than the Racket**

nnnnYou can buy the best racket in the world, but if you put stiff Polyester strings in it, you will still have pain.

nnnn
n- **Avoid:** Full bed of Poly (RPM Blast, Luxilon).nnnn- **Use:** Multifilament (Tecnifibre X-One Biphase, Wilson NXT). They fray like gut and feel soft.n
nnnn## **FAQs**

nnnn### **Can a racket cure tennis elbow?**

No racket can "cure" it (only rest and rehab do that), but the right racket can stop it from getting worse. A flexible frame reduces the shock that aggravates the tendon, allowing you to play longer without pain.### **Is a lighter racket better for arm pain?**

Not always. A racket that is *too* light (under 280g) will vibrate more upon impact with a heavy ball. A medium-weight racket (300-310g) absorbs more of the collision energy so your arm doesn't have to.### **What string tension is best for tennis elbow?**

Lower is better. High tension creates a "boardy," stiff feel. Drop your tension to **48–52 lbs**. This creates a trampoline effect that is softer on impact.nnnn## **Final Recommendations: My Personal Picks**
